My research is motivated by three broad questions:

1. **What is the nature of gravity and spacetime?**
2. **What is the nature of dark matter?**
3. **How fast is the Universe expanding?**

I address these questions using black-hole physics, gravitational waves, analytical and numerical relativity, and computational methods.

## What is the nature of gravity and spacetime?

General Relativity has passed all current experimental tests, but the strong-field regime of gravity remains comparatively unexplored. Black holes and gravitational waves provide a direct way to test gravity where spacetime is highly dynamical.

A major part of my research focuses on black-hole spacetimes, perturbations, and quasinormal modes in General Relativity and beyond. I develop spectral methods for computing rotating black-hole solutions and their oscillation spectra, with the aim of connecting gravitational theories to observable ringdown signals.

This work includes the development and application of **METRICS**, a spectral framework for studying black holes and their perturbations across a wide range of spins and gravitational theories.

I also work on gravitational-wave tests of General Relativity, including black-hole ringdown, compact-binary dynamics, and numerical-relativity simulations.

## What is the nature of dark matter?

Black holes can also probe light or ultralight fields that may be connected to the dark sector.

My work in this direction includes studying the effects of scalar and vector fields on black-hole spacetimes, quasinormal modes, and gravitational-wave signals. I am particularly interested in how dark-sector fields can modify the dynamics and observables of compact objects.

Current directions include black holes interacting with ultralight fields, extreme-mass-ratio inspirals in dark-sector environments, and binary-black-hole systems coupled to additional bosonic fields.

## How fast is the Universe expanding?

Gravitational waves provide an independent probe of cosmic expansion.

I work on methods for measuring the Hubble constant using gravitational-wave populations and stochastic gravitational-wave backgrounds. In particular, I am interested in combining information from compact-binary populations, unresolved gravitational-wave signals, and cosmological inference to improve measurements of the expansion rate of the Universe.

## Research areas

My research spans:

- Black holes and strong-field gravity
- Gravitational-wave tests of General Relativity
- Black-hole perturbations and quasinormal modes
- Spectral methods for gravitational physics
- Numerical relativity and compact-binary dynamics
- Dark-sector fields around compact objects
- Gravitational-wave cosmology
